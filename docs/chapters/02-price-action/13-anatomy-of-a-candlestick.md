# Anatomy of a Candlestick

> **In one line:** One candle is a small picture of a fight between buyers and sellers — learn its parts and you can read that fight.

## 🎯 What you'll learn
- What a candlestick (a small bar shape that shows price for one time period) is made of.
- The four price points every candle records: open, high, low, close.
- The difference between the body and the wicks.
- How to tell a bullish candle from a bearish one.
- What a long wick, a big body, a Doji, and a Spinning Top are telling you.

## 📘 Key concepts

### The four prices in one candle
Every candle covers a fixed time (like 1 day or 5 minutes). In that time it stores four prices.
- **Open** — where price started for the period. (First trade.)
- **Close** — where price ended for the period. (Last trade.)
- **High** — the highest price touched during the period.
- **Low** — the lowest price touched during the period.
- Think of a shop's day: the first sale price, the last sale price, the dearest sale, the cheapest sale.

### Body and wicks
The candle has two parts.
- **Body** — the thick rectangle. It shows the range between the open and the close. This is the "real range" — the true strength of the move.
- **Wicks** (also called shadows — the thin lines above and below the body). The **upper wick** reaches up to the high. The **lower wick** reaches down to the low.
- Bigger body = stronger momentum (a strong one-sided push).
- Longer wick = stronger rejection (price went there, but got pushed back). Long wicks also mean indecision or high volatility (price swinging fast).

### Bullish vs bearish (green vs red)
Colour tells you who won the period.
- **Bullish candle** (usually green): close is higher than open. Buyers won. Open sits at the bottom of the body, close at the top.
- **Bearish candle** (usually red): close is lower than open. Sellers won. Open sits at the top of the body, close at the bottom.
- Green = buyers in control. Red = sellers in control.

### Candle types at a glance
- **Bullish** — close > open. Buyers pushed price up.
- **Bearish** — close < open. Sellers pushed price down.
- **Doji** — open is about equal to close (tiny body). A tug-of-war with no winner. Indecision, and sometimes a warning of a reversal (a turn in direction).
- **Spinning Top** — small body with long wicks on both sides. Big swings but no clear winner. Also indecision or a possible reversal.

## 🔍 Example
Say a stock trades for one day.
- Open: ₹100
- Close: ₹110
- High: ₹112
- Low: ₹98

Close (₹110) is above open (₹100), so this is a **green (bullish)** candle.
- Body = ₹100 to ₹110 (a fat body → strong buying).
- Upper wick = ₹110 up to ₹112 (small).
- Lower wick = ₹100 down to ₹98 (small).

A tiny sketch:

```
   | 112  high
 ┌─┴─┐ 110 close
 │   │
 └─┬─┘ 100 open
   | 98   low
```

Now imagine a different day: open ₹100, close ₹101, but high ₹112 and low ₹90. Same rough area, but a tiny body with huge wicks — a Spinning Top. Buyers and sellers fought hard and nobody won. Same candle shape family, very different story.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Reading only the colour and ignoring the body size and wicks. Colour is just one clue.
- Thinking a long wick is "nothing". A long wick is a loud signal of rejection.
- Confusing the top of a red body: on a red candle the top is the **open**, not the close.
- Treating a single Doji as a sure reversal. It only hints — always check the candles around it.
- Forgetting the time frame. A "1-day candle" and a "5-minute candle" tell very different stories.

## ✅ Key takeaways
- A candle stores four prices: open, high, low, close.
- Body = the real range and strength; wicks = the extreme high and low.
- Green (bullish): close above open. Red (bearish): close below open.
- Bigger body = stronger momentum; longer wick = stronger rejection.
- Doji and Spinning Top both signal indecision or a possible turn.

## 📝 Quick check
1. **Q:** On a green (bullish) candle, where is the close?
   **A:** At the top of the body. Close is above open.
2. **Q:** What does a very long lower wick suggest?
   **A:** Strong rejection of lower prices — sellers pushed price down but buyers pushed it back up.
3. **Q:** What is a Doji telling you?
   **A:** Open and close are almost equal — indecision, and sometimes a warning of a reversal.

## 📖 New words
- **Candlestick** — a bar shape showing open, high, low, and close for one time period.
- **Body** — the thick part of the candle, the range between open and close.
- **Wick (shadow)** — the thin line reaching to the high (upper) or low (lower).
- **Bullish** — price closed higher than it opened; buyers in control.
- **Bearish** — price closed lower than it opened; sellers in control.
- **Rejection** — price reached a level but got pushed back away from it.
- **Reversal** — a change in the direction of the trend.
- **Doji** — a candle where open and close are almost equal (indecision).
- **Spinning Top** — a candle with a small body and long wicks on both sides (indecision).

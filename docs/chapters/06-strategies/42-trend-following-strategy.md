# Trend-Following Strategy

> **In one line:** Find a strong trend, wait for a calm dip, enter, and ride the move with a clear stop and target.

## 🎯 What you'll learn
- What "trend-following" (trading in the same direction the price is already moving) means.
- A simple 5-step rule set to spot, enter, and exit a trend.
- Where to place your stop-loss (a safety exit that limits your loss) and target.
- Why this works in strong trends and fails in choppy, sideways markets.

## 📘 Key concepts

### What a trend is
A trend is the general direction the price is walking. Think of a river — it flows one way.
- **Uptrend** — price makes higher highs (each peak is taller) and higher lows (each dip is shallower). Buyers are winning.
- **Downtrend** — lower highs and lower lows. Sellers are winning.
- **Sideways** — price wiggles in a flat range, no clear direction. Skip these.

### The trend-following idea
- Do not guess the top or bottom. Join the crowd already moving.
- It is like walking on a moving walkway at the airport — go the same way and you cover more ground with less effort.
- We use a tool called an **EMA** (Exponential Moving Average — a line that shows the average price of the last N days, with recent days counting more). A **50 EMA** is a common trend guide.

### The 5 rules (for an uptrend)
Follow these in order, like a checklist. Do not skip steps.
- **Rule 1 — Confirm the uptrend:** price makes higher highs and higher lows, and price sits above a rising 50 EMA.
- **Rule 2 — Wait for a pullback:** let the price dip back toward support (a price floor where buyers step in) or the 50 EMA. Do not chase a price that has run far already.
- **Rule 3 — Enter on a trigger:** wait for a bullish candle (a green candle showing buyers took control) or a bullish engulfing (a big green candle that fully covers the last red one) right at that level.
- **Rule 4 — Set the stop-loss:** place it just below the recent swing low (the last small dip). If price falls there, the trend idea is wrong — exit.
- **Rule 5 — Set the target or trail:** aim for the next resistance (a price ceiling where selling appears), or trail your stop (move it up as price rises) to ride the trend longer.

### When it works and when it does not
- Works best in a **strong, clean trend**. The walkway is moving fast.
- Struggles in **choppy, sideways markets** — you get faked out again and again. When in doubt, sit out.
- For a downtrend, flip the rules and short-sell (bet on a fall). But most beginners start **long-only** (buy only). That is fine.

## 🔍 Example
Stock "ABC" is in a clean uptrend, price above a rising 50 EMA.
- Price dips to the 50 EMA at ₹100 (Rule 2 — the pullback).
- A bullish candle forms there. You **enter (buy) at ₹100** (Rule 3).
- Recent swing low is ₹95, so **stop-loss at ₹95** (Rule 4). Risk = ₹5 per share.
- Next resistance is ₹115, so **target ₹115** (Rule 5). Reward = ₹15 per share.
- **Risk-reward = 5 : 15 = 1 : 3.** You risk ₹5 to try for ₹15.

```
Price
115 |          ● target
    |        /
100 |  ●----/   entry (dip to EMA)
 95 |  ---     stop (below swing low)
    +----------------> time
```
- If it hits ₹95 first, you lose ₹5 and walk away calmly. That is a normal cost of business.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- **Chasing** — buying after price has already jumped far, with no pullback. You pay too high.
- **Trading sideways markets** — forcing a trend trade when there is no trend.
- **No stop-loss** — hoping a losing trade turns around. Hope is not a plan.
- **Fighting the trend** — buying in a downtrend because it "looks cheap".
- **Breaking your own rules** — moving the stop lower to avoid a loss. Follow the plan mechanically.

## ✅ Key takeaways
- Trade **with** the trend, not against it.
- Only act when all rules line up: trend + pullback + trigger.
- Every trade needs a stop-loss before you enter.
- Aim for a reward bigger than your risk (like 1 : 2 or 1 : 3).
- Skip choppy markets — cash is a position too.

## 📝 Quick check
1. **Q:** In an uptrend, where do you wait to enter?
   **A:** On a pullback toward support or the 50 EMA, after a bullish trigger — not by chasing a price that already ran up.
2. **Q:** Where does the stop-loss go in this strategy?
   **A:** Just below the recent swing low, so you exit if the trend idea proves wrong.
3. **Q:** Why avoid sideways markets?
   **A:** There is no clear trend to ride, so you get faked out repeatedly.

## 📖 New words
- **Trend** — the general direction price is moving (up, down, or sideways).
- **Uptrend** — higher highs and higher lows; buyers in control.
- **EMA (Exponential Moving Average)** — an average-price line that weights recent prices more.
- **Pullback** — a small temporary dip against the trend.
- **Support** — a price floor where buyers tend to step in.
- **Resistance** — a price ceiling where sellers tend to appear.
- **Bullish candle / engulfing** — a green candle (or a big green one covering the last red) that signals buyers took control.
- **Stop-loss** — a pre-set exit that caps your loss.
- **Swing low** — the last small dip in price before it rose again.
- **Trailing stop** — a stop you move up as price rises, to lock in gains.
- **Short-sell** — a trade that profits if price falls.
- **Risk-reward** — the ratio of money risked to money aimed for.

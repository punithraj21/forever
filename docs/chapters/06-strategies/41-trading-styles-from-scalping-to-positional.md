# Trading Styles: Scalping to Positional

> **In one line:** Pick a trading style that fits your time, your calm, and your daily life — not the one that looks the most exciting online.

## 🎯 What you'll learn
- The four main trading styles, sorted by how long you hold a trade.
- How they differ in time, stress, screen time, and money needed.
- Why swing or positional is usually gentler for a beginner.
- How to match a style to your own life, not to hype.

## 📘 Key concepts

### The four styles by holding time
"Holding time" just means how long you keep a trade before selling. Think of it like fruit in a shop — some sells in minutes, some sits for weeks.

- **Scalping** (many tiny trades) — hold for seconds to minutes. You grab small moves, many times a day.
- **Intraday / day trading** (buy and sell same day) — you close every trade before the market shuts. No trade is left open overnight, so no surprise overnight risk (bad news while you sleep).
- **Swing trading** — hold for a few days to a few weeks. You try to catch one bigger move.
- **Positional / long-term** — hold for weeks, months, or more. This is the closest to investing.

### How they compare
Each style asks for a different amount of you.

- **Screen time** — scalping and intraday need you glued to the screen during market hours. Swing needs one or two checks a day. Positional needs the least.
- **Stress** — faster styles feel more like a fast cricket over: quick, tense, no rest. Slower styles feel calmer.
- **Costs matter most when fast** — every trade has costs (brokerage, taxes, fees). Scalpers trade so often that small costs add up fast, like paying an entry fee every time you step into the bazaar.
- **Capital** — the money you start with. You can begin any style small, but faster styles punish mistakes faster.
- **Chart timeframe** — the "zoom level" of your price chart. Scalpers watch 1-minute charts. Intraday uses 5 to 15 minutes. Swing uses hourly or daily. Positional uses daily or weekly.

### Which suits a beginner?
- Fast styles feel thrilling, but the speed leaves no time to think.
- Swing and positional give you hours or days to decide. Mistakes are slower and easier to fix.
- Most calm beginners do better starting slow.

## 🔍 Example
Meet Ravi. He has a full-time job from 9 to 6. He cannot watch charts all day.

- Scalping? No — he cannot stare at a 1-minute chart for hours.
- Intraday? No — the market is open while he is at work.
- Swing trading? Yes — he checks his phone once at lunch and once at night.

Ravi buys a share at ₹100. His plan: hold a few days, sell near ₹110, and exit at ₹95 if it falls (his stop-loss — the safety price where he accepts a small loss). After four days it reaches ₹109. He sells. Small, calm, done.

```
Style     Hold time    Screen time   Feels like
--------  -----------  ------------  ----------
Scalp     seconds/min  all day       sprint
Intraday  one day      market hours  fast run
Swing     days/weeks   1-2 checks    steady jog
Position  weeks+       rare checks   long walk
```

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Choosing scalping because a video made it look easy and rich.
- Trying intraday while holding a full-time job — you cannot watch the screen.
- Ignoring costs; fast trading eats profit through fees.
- Jumping between styles every week instead of learning one.
- Copying a style that fits someone else's life, not yours.

## ✅ Key takeaways
- Styles differ mainly by holding time: seconds to months.
- Faster styles need more screen time and bring more stress.
- Costs hurt fast traders the most.
- Beginners usually find swing or positional calmer and kinder.
- Match the style to your schedule and temperament, not to hype.

## 📝 Quick check
1. **Q:** Which style leaves no trade open overnight?
   **A:** Intraday (day trading) — every trade is closed the same day.
2. **Q:** You have a busy job and can only check twice a day. Which style fits best?
   **A:** Swing trading — a few days to a few weeks, with just one or two checks daily.
3. **Q:** Why do costs hurt scalpers the most?
   **A:** They make many trades, so small fees add up fast.

## 📖 New words
- **Holding time** — how long you keep a trade before selling.
- **Scalping** — many very short trades held for seconds to minutes.
- **Intraday / day trading** — buying and selling within the same market day.
- **Swing trading** — holding a trade for a few days to a few weeks.
- **Positional / long-term** — holding for weeks to months or more.
- **Overnight risk** — the danger of bad news while a trade is left open past the day.
- **Chart timeframe** — the zoom level of a price chart (e.g. 1-minute, daily).
- **Stop-loss** — a set price where you exit to accept a small, planned loss.
- **Capital** — the money you start trading with.

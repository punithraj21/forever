# Trading Psychology

> **In one line:** Your biggest opponent in trading is not the market — it is your own emotions, and the goal is to manage them, not to feel nothing.

## 🎯 What you'll learn
- The main emotions that hurt traders: fear, greed, FOMO, hope and revenge.
- Why a winning streak can be dangerous.
- Simple tools to stay calm and in control.
- Why "process" matters more than any single win or loss.

## 📘 Key concepts

### The market amplifies your feelings
The market is like a loud bazaar. It makes your emotions bigger.

- **FEAR** (worry of losing money) — makes you sell too early or freeze and not act at all.
- **GREED** (wanting more and more) — makes you buy a big size or hold a winner too long, until the profit turns into a loss.
- **FOMO** (Fear Of Missing Out — chasing a move that already happened) — you buy after the price already jumped, and often buy at the top.
- **HOPE** (praying a losing trade will come back) — you hold a bad trade instead of taking a small loss, and the loss grows.
- **REVENGE** (trading angrily right after a loss) — you try to "win it back" fast, take a wild bet, and lose more.

### Two hidden traps
These feel good but are risky.

- **Overconfidence after wins** — after 3 or 4 wins in a row you feel like a genius. You take a bigger risk, and one bad trade wipes out all the wins.
- **Overtrading** (taking too many trades) — you trade out of boredom, just to feel busy. More trades means more fees and more mistakes.

### Tools to stay in control
You cannot delete emotions. But you can build fences around them.

- **Trade a written plan and checklist** — decide your rules before the market opens. This removes hot decisions made in the heat of the moment.
- **Use proper position sizing** (how much money you put in one trade) — risk only a small amount, like 1% of your account. Small risk means small fear.
- **Keep a trading journal** — write each trade AND how you felt. Patterns become clear.
- **Take breaks and set a daily loss limit** — after 2 losses, stop for the day. Walk away. Come back fresh.
- **Focus on process, not outcome** — a good trade can still lose, and a bad trade can still win by luck. Judge yourself on following your rules, not on one result.

## 🔍 Example

Riya starts with ₹1,00,000. Her plan: risk only 1% (₹1,000) per trade.

She loses two trades in a row (−₹2,000). She feels angry and wants revenge.

Instead of following her plan, she thinks: *"I'll bet big and win it all back."*

```
Plan says:   risk ₹1,000  (1%)
Revenge says: risk ₹20,000 (20%)  ← FEAR + GREED talking
```

If she follows revenge and loses, she is down ₹22,000 — hard to recover.

Riya instead closes her laptop, takes a walk, and stops for the day. Next morning she is calm and back to her ₹1,000 rule. She protected her account. That is a **win** — even though today ended red.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Buying only because the price is "going up fast" (that is FOMO).
- Holding a loser and hoping, instead of taking a planned small loss.
- Trading bigger after a few wins because you feel unbeatable.
- Taking a "revenge trade" right after a loss to get even.
- Trading all day out of boredom, with no setup or plan.

## ✅ Key takeaways
- Your emotions, not the market, are your real opponent.
- Fear, greed, FOMO, hope and revenge each push you into bad choices.
- A written plan and small position size remove most of the pressure.
- A journal, breaks and loss limits keep you steady.
- Judge the process, not one win or loss.

## 📝 Quick check
1. **Q:** What is FOMO, and why is it risky?
   **A:** Fear Of Missing Out — chasing a move that already happened. You often buy at the top, after the easy gain is gone.
2. **Q:** You lost two trades and feel angry. What is the smart move?
   **A:** Stop for the day. Do not take a revenge trade. Come back calm tomorrow.
3. **Q:** Why focus on process instead of outcome?
   **A:** Because a good trade can lose and a bad trade can win by luck. Following your rules is what you can control.

## 📖 New words
- **FEAR** — worry of losing money (or of missing a chance) that makes you act badly.
- **GREED** — wanting more and more, so you risk too much or hold too long.
- **FOMO** — Fear Of Missing Out; chasing a price move that already happened.
- **HOPE** — holding a losing trade and praying it recovers instead of exiting.
- **REVENGE trading** — trading angrily to win back a loss.
- **Overtrading** — taking too many trades, often out of boredom.
- **Position sizing** — how much money you put at risk in one trade.
- **Trading journal** — a diary of your trades and the feelings behind them.

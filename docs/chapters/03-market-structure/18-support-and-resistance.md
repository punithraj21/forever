# Support & Resistance

> **In one line:** Price often stops falling at a floor (support) and stops rising at a ceiling (resistance) — and these levels help you plan your trades.

## 🎯 What you'll learn
- What support and resistance (S/R) mean in simple words.
- Why these levels form again and again.
- How old floors become ceilings, and old ceilings become floors.
- How to draw S/R and how to use them.

## 📘 Key concepts

### What is support and resistance?
S/R (support and resistance) are price zones where the market often changes direction.
- **Support** = a price floor. Here buyers (people who want to buy) tend to step in. Their buying can stop the price from falling more.
- **Resistance** = a price ceiling. Here sellers (people who want to sell) tend to step in. Their selling can stop the price from rising more.
- Think of a ball in a room. It bounces off the floor and stops at the ceiling. Price acts the same way.

### Why these levels form
Levels form because price has a memory. Many orders (buy/sell requests) gather at the same spots.
- People remember, "Last time it stopped at ₹100 and went up." So they buy near ₹100 again.
- Others think, "It kept failing at ₹150." So they sell near ₹150 again.
- **Round numbers** (like 100, 500, 1000) act as natural magnets. Our minds like clean numbers.
- More touches = a stronger level. A price the market tested many times matters more.

### Role reversal (the flip)
This is very important. When a level breaks, it often swaps its job.
- Old **resistance**, once the price closes above it, often becomes new **support**.
- Old **support**, once the price closes below it, often becomes new **resistance**.
- Like a shop's roof. If you climb above the roof, that same roof is now the floor you stand on.

### S/R are zones, not exact lines
- Do not treat a level as one perfect number. Treat it as a small band, or **zone** (a small price range).
- Example: instead of exactly ₹200, think "the ₹198 to ₹202 area."
- Price is messy. Give the level a little room to breathe.

## 🔍 Example
Say a stock, "Sunrise Ltd," trades like this:
- It falls to ₹100 three times and bounces up each time. So ₹100 is **support**.
- It rises to ₹120 twice and drops back each time. So ₹120 is **resistance**.

A tiny picture:

```
₹120 ---X---X---  <- resistance (ceiling)
       /   \  /
      /     \/
₹100 -o---o---o-  <- support (floor)
```

How you might use it:
- Near ₹100, buyers may return, so you watch for a **buy** chance.
- Near ₹120, sellers may return, so you watch to book profit or stay away.
- Now imagine the price closes above ₹120. The flip may happen: ₹120 could become new **support**. If price later dips back to ₹120 and holds, that is the old ceiling acting as a floor.

Remember: this is a plan, not a promise. Levels can and do break.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- **Blindly trusting a bounce.** A level can break. Never assume price must turn.
- **Drawing lines too thin.** S/R is a zone. An exact single price will trap you.
- **Ignoring round numbers.** Beginners often miss 100, 500, 1000 — but the market watches them.
- **Using weak levels.** A level touched only once is weak. More touches means stronger.
- **No exit plan.** Even a strong level can fail, so always know where you are wrong and will exit.

## ✅ Key takeaways
- Support is a floor where buyers step in; resistance is a ceiling where sellers step in.
- Levels form because price has memory and orders cluster there.
- Broken resistance can become support, and broken support can become resistance (the flip).
- Draw S/R from past swing highs and lows, and watch round numbers.
- Treat S/R as zones, and never assume a bounce — levels can break.

## 📝 Quick check
1. **Q:** What is the difference between support and resistance?
   **A:** Support is a price floor where buyers tend to step in; resistance is a price ceiling where sellers tend to step in.
2. **Q:** A stock breaks above old resistance at ₹120. What might that level become?
   **A:** It may flip and become new support — the old ceiling can act as a new floor.
3. **Q:** Why should you treat S/R as a zone, not one exact price?
   **A:** Because price is messy and rarely stops at a perfect number, so you give the level a small range of room.

## 📖 New words
- **Support** — a price floor where buyers tend to step in and slow a fall.
- **Resistance** — a price ceiling where sellers tend to step in and slow a rise.
- **S/R** — short for support and resistance.
- **Buyers** — people placing orders to buy.
- **Sellers** — people placing orders to sell.
- **Orders** — buy or sell requests sent to the market.
- **Zone** — a small price range treated as one level, not a single exact price.
- **Role reversal (flip)** — when a broken support becomes resistance, or a broken resistance becomes support.
